# Chance and the Cost of Physics: Structural Accounting Under Entropy

## What is this?
A foundational physics artifact in the Structural Flow project.

This paper applies one structural constraint across several familiar physics and cosmology problems:

**history is not free.**

It examines double-slit observation, entanglement, wavefunction collapse, photon propagation, dark matter, and nothingness through that accounting lens.

## Why should I care?
Many explanations in physics quietly assume that persistence, memory, or integration come at no cost.

This artifact asks what changes when those assumptions are withdrawn.

If you care about quantum weirdness, cosmology, or the structural price of having a past, this paper gives a compact alternative frame.

## What is the claim?
The paper’s central claim is not a new mechanism or particle model.

The claim is narrower:

physical explanation often smuggles in costless history, costless memory, or costless integration, and some familiar paradoxes look different once those costs are put back into the ledger.

## What is this not?
This artifact is not:
- a new particle theory
- a replacement for empirical physics
- a final canonical statement of the full later physics program
- a claim that every downstream section here remains the latest formulation

It is preserved as a live origin-layer bridge artifact in the physics program.

## How was this done?
The paper uses Structural Flow as a minimal structural grammar and applies it uniformly across familiar problem domains.

It does not try to explain mechanisms.
It tries to account for what must be paid for if history, memory, and distinction are real.
